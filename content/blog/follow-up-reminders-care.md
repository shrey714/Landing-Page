Many patients leave the clinic with advice, medicines, lab tests, or a revisit plan. The problem is that follow-up tasks often depend on manual memory, paper notes, or phone calls.

A structured follow-up workflow helps the clinic team know which patients need attention next and why.

## Where follow-ups get missed

Follow-ups can get missed when:

- Advice is written but not tracked.
- Lab reports come back but no one reviews them.
- Patients forget revisit dates.
- Reception does not know which follow-ups are pending.
- Doctors do not have a quick view of upcoming patient tasks.

## What a follow-up workflow should do

A good follow-up workflow should:

- Capture the follow-up reason during consultation.
- Store the due date.
- Show pending follow-ups to the right team.
- Connect the follow-up to the patient record.
- Help staff prioritize patients who need attention.

## Better continuity, less manual work

Follow-up reminders are not just about sending messages. They help the clinic team maintain continuity of care by keeping the next action visible.

This reduces missed revisits, improves patient communication, and gives doctors a clearer view of ongoing care.

## Start with the most important follow-ups

Clinics can start by tracking:

- Lab report reviews
- Repeat consultations
- Chronic care check-ins
- Post-treatment reviews
- Pending patient calls
